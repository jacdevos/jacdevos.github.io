People are afraid of AI—even some of the people building it. There are real risks, but we need a better mental model of what large language models actually are.

In Terry Pratchett's *Truckers* (1989), tiny people called Nomes carry a mysterious black box, **the Thing**, passed down as a treasured cultural artifact. One day it starts talking. They discover it's a computer containing knowledge of their own forgotten civilisation. What seemed alien was theirs all along.

LLMs can feel just as mysterious. But their intelligence wasn't simply programmed into a clever algorithm. The breakthrough was finding a way to learn from vast amounts of information—and discovering that **the more these models learned at scale, the more capable they became**.

Much of that information came from the internet: writing, ideas, explanations and discoveries contributed by ordinary people from all walks of life. The models don't literally store all that knowledge, but they learn patterns from it and can apply them to our questions and problems.

**We, the people, supplied much of what makes these systems intelligent. LLMs let us interact with patterns of our collective intelligence. They are cultural artifacts, not alien visitors.**

That doesn't erase the risks. It changes how we understand them.

**My recommendation:** read *Truckers* with your family, especially your kids. Then consider our own Thing—and embrace it as a cultural artifact, something of ourselves to understand and use thoughtfully.
